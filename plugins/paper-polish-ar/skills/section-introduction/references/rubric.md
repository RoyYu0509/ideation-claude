# Introduction — rubric

Acceptance criteria for the Introduction. Verdict + evidence per item. Overall structure:
**funnel + contradiction-driven + contribution-landing** — broad background, then existing
progress, then a specific deep bottleneck, why existing methods can't naturally solve it, the
new idea/framework/mechanism, key results that support it, and the broader significance.

## Paragraph functions
Each paragraph carries one clear function; together they form the funnel:
background → progress → gap → difficulty → our method → key results → significance.

## Seven-sentence latent logic (a guide, not a template to copy verbatim)
- Broad problem is central to a major goal.
- Recent advances have enabled a current capability.
- However, a specific challenge remains an obstacle.
- This difficulty arises because of a structural reason.
- Here we develop/introduce a new framework that does X.
- We establish/demonstrate key results with quantitative/theoretical evidence.
- Together, these provide a new principle/tool/route for a broader class of problems.

## The five must-answer questions (the core test)
A finished Introduction must clearly answer all five. Score each:
1. **Why is the problem important — to the field, not to the author?**
2. **Why is existing work insufficient — a clear, specific bottleneck, not merely "nobody did it"?**
3. **Why is the bottleneck hard — a deep reason at the mechanism / theory / algorithm / experiment
   level?**
4. **Why is your method natural and necessary — does the reader, on reaching it, feel "yes, it
   should be done this way"?**
5. **Why do the results have broader meaning — a generalizable principle / framework / paradigm,
   not a single case study?**

## Mapping and content checks
- The gap is **specific**, not generic.
- Each claimed contribution maps **one-to-one** to a stated gap.
- Key results carry quantitative metrics, theoretical guarantees, or a key mechanism — not bare
  "novelty" assertions.

## Anti-patterns (flag)
- Literature-review-style listing of prior work.
- Empty / generic novelty claims.
- Vague gap; contributions not mapped to gaps.
- Template seams visible (every gap with "However", mechanical paragraph openers — see ai-tells).
