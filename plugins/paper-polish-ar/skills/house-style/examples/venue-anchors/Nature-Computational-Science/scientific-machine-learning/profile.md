# Venue profile — Nature Computational Science / scientific-machine-learning

## identity
- venue: Nature Computational Science (Nature-family, broad-audience)
- field: scientific machine learning
- type: broad-audience flagship-family journal (NOT a specialist methods journal)
- audience: any computational or quantitative scientist; the first paragraph must be readable by a scientist outside the immediate subfield

## register
Accessible, significance-forward, and narrative, while staying precise and rigorous. Unlike a
specialist methods journal, the prose leads with the question and why it matters broadly, then the
finding, then the mechanism explained so a non-specialist can follow it. Far less equation-dense than
JCP: keep heavy derivations and numerical detail for Methods / SI and let the main text carry the
idea, the evidence, and the implication. Confident and concrete; no hype, no empty novelty, but the
broad significance is stated plainly (it is expected here, not optional).

## vocabulary
### venue-acceptable in moderation
- significant / significantly — statistical or clearly-quantified sense OK; flag as a vague intensifier.
- robust / robustness — technical sense OK; flag when vague.
- broad-reach framing ("of broad relevance to", "across disciplines") — acceptable in moderation when
  the paper genuinely supports it; flag when it is unsupported reach rather than a real implication.
### venue-stricter
- equation-dense or jargon-first openings — stricter than JCP: a section opening that needs in-field
  notation to parse fails the accessibility bar here.

## conventions
- spelling: British / Oxford (match the journal's current house style)
- abstract: unstructured single paragraph, ~150-200 words, broad-audience opener then "Here we show"; NO labeled headings. (Verify per target: Nature flagship caps a summary paragraph near 150 words; some npj journals accept a standard structured abstract.)
- title: short, declarative, finding- or capability-forward, readable by non-specialists; avoid method jargon in the title
- references: numbered superscript (Nature style); main-text reference count is limited
- sections: Introduction (no heading) -> Results -> Discussion -> Methods AT THE END (Nature-family order; Methods is not inline/early as in JCP)
- math/display: minimal in the main text; full derivations and numerical detail go to Methods / SI
- figures: a small number of main display items (typically up to ~4-6), each broad-audience legible; an overview/schematic figure is common

## acceptance-bar
The detailed form for the /review panel lives in review/. Summary, also used as the keynote effort prior:
- reviewers reward: broad scientific significance and cross-field interest; a genuinely novel advance; technically sound, well-controlled evidence; accessibility for non-specialists; reproducibility.
- reject triggers: an advance that is solid but field-internal / incremental (no broad interest); novelty not distinguished from prior work; a central claim not established by the evidence; prose only a specialist can follow.
- novelty / significance bar: HIGH and BROAD. Unlike JCP, field-internal usefulness is not enough; the work must matter beyond the immediate subfield and the paper must make that case.
- panel kit: review/rubric.md + review/reviewer-profiles.md + review/scoring.md.

## limits
- abstract ~150-200 words (confirm the exact cap for the target journal); main-text length and display-item counts are journal-capped — check the author guide.
